# Replay check: v2 over the night of 2026-10-14/15

SPEC section 17.2. Build: `feat/factory-loop-v2` at 901a0a8, read from a scratch copy (`git archive`).
The checkout at `.treehouse/dev-system-22f535/8/dev-system` was not changed (`git status` clean after).
Window: 2026-10-14 16:20 to 2026-10-15 07:22 UTC, device `coder-dev-system-loop`.

## The answers

- **Timeouts.** v2 never left an active issue past its state timeout without a step. In 13 simulated runs (13,460
  passes), every record in an active state had a running step, a planned action, or a skip line saying why it waited.
  No pass had a record with none of these. Every wait longer than the state timeout was a hold with a named skip
  line. Almost all were the usage tier (`tier stop-all`, 23:03 to 02:20). One was the 30-minute step breaker.
  Held steps spend no attempt, as the spec says (property 11). Agents that hung were cut off at
  their timeout plus one pass: for example, a design started 18:47:05 timed out at 19:32:55
  (`sim/runs/faults-k3-s1/transitions.jsonl`).
- **Branches and PRs.** v2 did not move a branch, open a second PR, or update a PR that was not the merge-queue head.
  The checks found zero cases in every run:
  - no `queue-update` or `queue-merge` was planned or run for a PR that was not the queue head;
  - every agent step, slot prepare and PR ensure used the record's own branch;
  - no record's branch or PR number changed;
  - no issue got more than one PR;
  - no `pr-close`, `update-behind`, `guard` or other v1 merge-flow action was ever planned.

  The stock replay agrees. Over 810 real states, v2 planned only `claim`, `label`, `stop-agent` and `sweep`. On the
  same states, v1 planned `pr-close` 1,765 times, `guard` 1,120 times and `spawn` 1,108 times
  (`replay/v1/replay.json`, `replay/v2/replay.json`).
- **Ghosts.** Yes, v2 releases them, and fast, but it releases every v1 claim, not only the ghosts:
  - At 02:40 and at 07:22, R1 names exactly the seven issues that held the cap overnight: #467, #571, #589, #590,
    #591, #613 and #620. It plans each release on its first pass.
  - In the takeover run, which starts v2 on v1's real 02:40 state, all seven were released 10 seconds after
    start.
  - None of the seven was labelled `ready`, so all seven left the factory as `abandoned`. That includes the two
    finished green PRs, #621 (#467) and #631 (#571).
  - R1 cannot tell a ghost from live v1 work. At 18:30 it names #356, #467, #533, #571 and #583, which were all
    being worked then. Across the night's samples it plans 529 `stop-agent` actions against v1's running agents.
  - This is the spec's rule (6.2: a live claim with no record is a ghost). It makes draining v1, or running the
    migration in act mode, a hard precondition of switching the planner (17.5).
- **The overnight stall (02:40 on).** The stall does not happen in a v2 world.
  - **v2 from cutover:** at 02:40 v2 was busy:
    - #589 merged at 02:30;
    - #620 was being designed;
    - #592's build was being re-run free after the 429;
    - #613's build answered "cannot" at 02:46, went to triage and parked at 02:49 with its question.

    After 03:33 nothing was active. Three issues were parked with questions for the owner (#590, #613, #624).
    The only queued issue was #628, which the `only` list excludes. Parked records never held capacity. The
    pure run had 9 passes where the cap looked full with fewer than K records active. In every one, a record
    closed or parked in that same pass.
  - **v2 dropped into v1's 02:40 state:**
    - It releases the seven, then claims #533 and #592 and merges both (02:51 and 03:23).
    - If the owner re-adds `ready` to the seven, v2 adopts their open PRs and merges five issues: #467 and
      #571 (the two that waited for a release), #589, #591 and #620. The last merge is at 04:31.
    - #590 and #613 park with their questions.
    - No release holds a merge.
- **Crashes and nonsense.** planV2 never threw: not on 810 real states, and not in 13,460 simulated passes.
  It planned no action outside the v2 kinds. One defect:
  - **Misleading skip line.** `rules-claim.ts:212` writes "owner: only names it, but it is not in the ready queue"
    for every issue in `only` that is not queued. That includes issues being worked and issues already merged.
    v1 limited the line to unclaimed issues (`plan.ts:2118-2121`, `stage === null || stage === 'queued'`).
  - Over the night that is 1,100 to 2,350 such lines per run on issues that had a record
    (`onlyLinesOnRecords` in each `summary.json`).
  - It hides nothing: the liveness check above ignores S1 lines. But it puts a false reason in the decision
    log for in-flight work. The fix is to keep v1's condition, or to skip issues that have a live record.

## Should v2 be trusted on this evidence?

The simulation is useful on the safety properties above, but it favours v2. Read the throughput numbers as an upper
bound:

- In the pure model, v2 claims 18 issues, merges 14, splits 1 (#600 into #602 and #603) and parks 3 with
  questions. The real night merged 4 of 17.
- In the eight fault-injection runs it merges 11 to 14.
- **The cutover bar is tight even here.** Of the first 10 claims, 6 merged within 3 hours untouched (K=3 and K=2).
  Three things count against it:
  - a flaky-check re-run on #571 counts as a loop intervention (`ci-rerun`);
  - a conflict fixer on #596 counts too (`fix:conflict`);
  - #589's merge fell into the 3 h 17 m usage window.

  With random faults the count drops to 1 to 4. The owner may want to know before cutover that normal
  queue mechanics count as "touched" under the bar.

## What the replay cannot show

- **Agent behaviour.** Design, build, review and fix outcomes and their durations are assumed, mapped from G1's
  per-issue table. The fault runs only draw them at random. The replay cannot show:
  - agents writing to the main checkout;
  - background polling loops;
  - wrong branches named in answers;
  - poor briefs, or a reviewer that misses bugs or invents findings.
- **The companion plugin.** The write guard, the 10-minute Bash timeout and the stop gate never ran. The run
  assumes they work.
- **The executors and observe.** The simulation runs actions at the outcome level and builds the State directly. It
  does not exercise `act.ts`, the real argv, the store, event-note encoding, `observe.ts` or the source reads. Unit
  tests cover those. The fault drills on `cbundy/dev-system-scratch` should too.
- **GitHub.** The model is simplified:
  - `update-branch`, mergeable states, CI timing (fixed at 3 minutes), patch ids and conflicts are modelled;
  - the 19:42 token expiry, rate limits and slow mergeable computation are absent.
- **The cause of most lost time.** no-mistakes is out of v2's path by design, so its failures (about 755
  issue-minutes, G1) cannot happen here. The owner gates (release holds) are also gone by design. That is the
  replay favouring the new design, not evidence that it handles them.
- **Mixed fleets.** Two loops on two devices, a v1 and v2 handover with agents still running, and the shadow mode.
- **The stock harness's v2 mode proves little on its own.** `--planner v2` plans each real state with v2's fields
  empty (`scripts/factory-loop-replay.js:229`), which causes three problems:
  - every v1 claim is a ghost to v2;
  - capacity never binds;
  - no heartbeat is planned.

  Its claim agreement (9 of 17, against v1's 7 of 17) is therefore not evidence of better claiming. It mostly
  shows that both planners apply the same filters. Both runs used an empty control file, so `only` and
  `noNewWork` were not applied.
- **The spec's own replay harness is not built.** That is `scripts/factory-loop-v2-replay.js` (SPEC 17.2), and
  the branch does not have it. The scratch simulation below stands in for it. It could be the starting point.

## How it was run

Everything is under `/persist/claude/factory-loop-v2-build/verify/replay/`.

- **The stock harness, both planners.** Run with `--planner v1` and `--planner v2`, `--step-minutes 2` over
  16:20 to 07:22, from a scratch copy. The only change to the copy is a read cache, so both runs saw identical
  inputs (`harness-cache.diff`). Outputs are in `v1/` and `v2/` (`replay.json`, `comparison.json`, logs):
  - v1: 810 samples, 177 live decisions, 117 agreed.
  - v2: 810 samples, 177 live decisions, 9 agreed (claims only, by construction).
- **The world simulation.** `sim/v2-sim.mjs` drives the real `planV2` pass by pass (60 s, 10 s after a pass that
  acted). It works over a simulated world:
  - **What it keeps:** the ledger, through `ledger.ts` `start`/`finish`; records, through `act-state.ts`
    `recordStale`/`applied`; and leases, PRs, CI, main and labels.
  - **The recorded night it replays:**
    - the owner's control changes, from the operator transcript;
    - the ready times, from the factory queue;
    - 8 outside merges to main, from `git log --first-parent`;
    - restarts at 19:43:28 and 22:03:03;
    - the session-limit 429 at 23:06:35, with its reset at 02:20;
    - usage tiers, from the factory's usage events.
  - **It checks every pass:** head-only, branch and PR identity, one PR per issue, capacity, one step per record,
    silence, idle time over each state's timeout, and crashes.

  Runs are in `sim/runs/<name>/`, each with `summary.json`, `passes.jsonl`, `transitions.jsonl` and `world.log`:
  - `pure-k3`, `pure-k2`: G1's outcomes mapped onto v2 steps;
  - `rescues-k3`: plus the person drops and closes;
  - `faults-k3-s1` to `s8`: random agent crashes, hangs, review changes, red CI, conflicts and declined fixes;
  - `takeover-0240`, `takeover-0240-reready`: v2 started on v1's real 02:40 state.

  The command is `node sim/v2-sim.mjs --src <scratch copy> --replay replay/v1/replay.json --out <dir> --k 3
  --variant pure|rescues|faults [--seed N] [--from ISO --takeover replay/v2/replay.json --reready N,...]`.
